A. Thapar plant data → cleaning plan

Plant data→Weather→Clean data→Expected energy→PR & soiling→Model choice→Cost optimisation→Downtime→Plan
  1. Collect plant data. Exported from the Solar Link monitoring portal (plant FP-924): daily and 5-minute inverter energy, pyranometer irradiance, plant capacity. SCADA exportinverter-level data
  2. Add weather. Rain, temperature and wind from ERA5; dust (PM10) from CAMS – free, peer-reviewed reanalysis datasets for Thapar's exact coordinates. ERA5CAMS
  3. Clean the data. Remove 12 impossible logger "catch-up" days, flag outages, treat the irradiance channel correctly as horizontal (GHI). data quality
  4. Expected energy. Sun position → sunlight on the tilted panel (plane-of-array) → cell temperature (NOCT model) → power with γ = −0.29 %/°C and 0.96 system losses.
  5. Performance. PR = Yf ÷ Yr (IEC 61724-1). Example 15 Oct: 3.67 ÷ 4.21 = 87.2 %. Performance index = actual ÷ expected.
  6. Soiling ratio. Heavy rain (≥ 8 mm) washes panels → that day is the "clean" reference. Output drop since then = dust loss. soiling rationatural cleaning events
  7. Choose the dust model honestly. Several models compared by leave-one-out cross-validation, then an independent audit with 3 scenarios: A 0.05, B 0.10, C 0.258 %/day.
  8. Cost optimisation. Best cleaning interval T* = √(2C ÷ (p·E·r)) → clean every 31 dry days, 4 rounds/yr, net ₹91,011/yr; break-even ₹10.70 per module. economic optimisationsensitivity analysisMonte Carlo
  9. Downtime detection. Each inverter vs the campus median; below 50 % = outage. Found 81,735 kWh (₹6.33 lakh) lost in 295 days – 8× the dust saving. anomaly detection
  10. Plan & dashboard. 14-day forecast → which block to clean, when, and what it is worth; live map, CSV exports.

B. Panel photo → dirt score → action

Upload→Pre-process→Find panels→Flatten→Pixel score P→Vision grade V→Fuse S→Thermal→Action + PDF
  1. Upload. Phone, drone or thermal photos (JPG, HEIC, FLIR/DJI radiometric R-JPEG, temperature CSV).
  2. Pre-process. Detect image type; for thermal files read the real temperatures embedded in the file (Planck conversion).
  3. Find every panel. A pretrained vision-language model used zero-shot outlines each panel (4 corners) and names dirt and defects. Big drone shots are split into overlapping tiles.
  4. Flatten. OpenCV refines each outline and applies a perspective transform so every panel is analysed face-on.
  5. Pixel score P (our algorithm). Mask the cell grid lines → find the clean-glass colour (k-means in CIELAB) → colour difference per pixel (ΔE2000) → coverage, opacity, contrast loss, deposits → P = 100·(1 − e−z).
  6. Vision grade V. The model's own 0–100 grade plus dirt type (dust, bird droppings, lichen…) and defects (cracks, burn marks).
  7. Fuse. S = 0.7·P + 0.3·V. Measurement dominates; if P and V differ by > 25 points the panel is flagged for a second look. sensor fusionexplainable AI
  8. Classify & act. Clean < 20 · partly soiled 20–55 · heavily soiled > 55 → no action / wash within N days / spot-clean now / maintenance.
  9. Thermal hotspots. Hotspot if ΔT ≥ 10 K above the panel median (IEC TS 62446-3).
  10. Output. Annotated image, per-panel table, saved inspection, PDF report.

Likely professor questions

Q. Why a cloud model? Why not train your own (e.g. YOLO)?

Training needs thousands of labelled photos of our panels with dirt marked by hand – no such Thapar dataset exists, and a small one would overfit. A pretrained model works zero-shot today. We did not hand it the decision: our own pixel measurement carries 70 % of the score, the thermal rule is a standard, and every number is explainable. Next step: the inspections we save become a labelled dataset to fine-tune a small on-device model.

Q. What is your own contribution then?

The whole pipeline: panel geometry and flattening, the ΔE2000 soiling algorithm, the fusion and disagreement check, the IEC thermal rule, the cleaning economics, the downtime detector, the ESP32→cloud link and the dashboard. The model is one component, like using a library.

Q. Why 0.7 and 0.3?

A design choice: measured pixels are objective and repeatable, so they get most weight; the vision grade adds context (type of dirt, defects). Calibrating these weights on labelled field photos is listed as future work.

Q. How accurate is it?

Checked on public, licensed solar images: clean drone panels score 2–12, visibly dusty panels 40–75, droppings and hotspots are found. A formal accuracy figure needs a labelled Thapar set – that is our validation plan, we do not claim a number.

Q. Is the plant data real?

Yes – exported from the plant's own monitoring portal, plus public ERA5/CAMS weather. Dust % and savings are model estimates built on that data and are labelled as such.

Q. Why did savings fall from ₹3.65 lakh to ₹0.91 lakh?

Our own audit showed the old dust rate came from the detector, not the dust. Real rain-wash data caps dust near 2–3 %. We corrected it – and found downtime (₹7.8 lakh/yr) is the bigger lever.
